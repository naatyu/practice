# AI infrastructure engineering study plan

Research snapshot, 19 November 2026. The [hiring evidence](jobs.md) contains individual postings, and the [release and systems evidence](reports.md) links the technical reports, model cards and engineering notes behind this plan.

## Where to focus

**Train a decoder you can profile, scale and recover. Pair that work with GPU kernels that appear in the training or serving path.** The repo already has decoder training, cached and speculative decoding, grouped MoE, and two-rank CPU/Gloo expert-parallel forward and backward exercises. The biggest gap is operating and diagnosing a complete workload across memory, communication, storage and failure boundaries.

The employer-board pass screened 4,552 listings across 20 feeds. The focused sample has **91 postings across 17 employers**, plus nine direct career-page checks from five additional employers. The split is 29 training, 31 inference, 22 fleet/networking, seven kernels, and two dedicated data roles before the supplements. Training has the most weight in the curriculum. The posting sample keeps adjacent roles visible so the choice of specialization is informed.

| Signal | Postings / employers in the 91-role sample | What to take from it |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| GPU communication or interconnects | 76 / 15 | Learn collective ordering, topology, slow-rank diagnosis and overlap. The broad match also picks up infrastructure descriptions. |
| Reliability, recovery or observability | 56 / 15 | Build measurements and failure recovery into the exercises. A fast step alone does not show that a job can recover. |
| Orchestration and fleet tooling | 53 / 15 | Learn scheduling and observability in the context of GPU jobs if you want the fleet track. |
| Profiling or benchmarking | 44 / 15 | Learn to read a trace and explain the bottleneck before changing code. |
| Storage, loading or I/O | 39 / 12 | Study data and checkpoint movement as part of the training runtime. |
| GPU kernel implementation or tooling | 31 / 14 | Keep a kernel track, especially for performance roles. |
| Inference runtimes, caching or batching | 28 / 11 | Build a scheduler and cache manager around your decoder. |
| Sharding or parallelism | 16 / 8 | Direct postings and model reports give much more depth than title or keyword counts. |
| Activation recomputation or offload | 0 automated matches | Keep it high priority. A supplemental Microsoft role names recomputation and systems reports explain why it is useful. |

The counts are descriptive of a targeted, keyword-screened sample. They mix responsibilities, qualification language and team context. They do not establish relative job-market demand or how commonly teams use an unnamed technique. Data and fleet roles are also under- or over-represented depending on how companies name their teams. Role-level examples and tag definitions are in [jobs.md](jobs.md).

## Implementation sequence

1. **Profile the current decoder and finish a CUDA correctness harness.** Compile standalone kernels independently, check launch and synchronization errors, and compare with a CPU/PyTorch reference. Measure one training step with fixed batch/sequence/dtype, warmup and a synchronized timing window. Record tokens/s, peak allocated and reserved memory, and time by major phase. Use Nsight Systems to find the slow region and Nsight Compute to investigate a chosen kernel. Treat the scan/max/Hadamard/transpose exercises as practice inputs, with compilation and GPU comparisons as separate checks. [Nsight Compute triage guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ComputeTriage/)
2. **Build activation checkpointing into a small dense training run.** Compare eager BF16 training with block checkpointing. Verify seeded dropout and input/parameter gradient parity, then sweep sequence length and checkpointed layers. Account for repeat work, peak allocated memory and step time. Repeat with MoE after making the snapshot window and router metrics safe under recomputation. Use a same-workload baseline and report any larger workload that only fits with checkpointing separately. This topic earns a place even with no automated job matches. DeepSeek explicitly discusses flexible rematerialization and the EUROPA team describes the limits of retaining only block-boundary tensors at long sequence lengths. [PyTorch checkpoint API](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/main/utils/checkpoint.html) · [DeepSeek-V4 §3.4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348) · [EUROPA/JUPITER run](https://pytorch.org/blog/training-a-1t-parameter-moe-on-eu-public-infrastructure-with-pytorch-and-torchtitan/)
3. **Build a trainer that resumes correctly and feeds the GPU.** Use deterministic token shards, document-aware packing, and a resumable sampler. Record valid tokens, dataset position, optimizer/scheduler state and RNG. Kill and resume at a known checkpoint, compare the next batches and parameters with an uninterrupted run, then profile prefetching and checkpoint stalls. Add rank-aware checkpointing and resharded load as a follow-up. This is distinct from activation checkpointing. [OpenAI Data Movement role](https://openai.com/careers/technical-lead-training-runtime-data%28set%29-movement-san-francisco/) · [PyTorch Distributed Checkpoint](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/main/distributed/checkpoint.html)
4. **Scale the training step.** Start with two CPU processes for gradient parity using unequal valid-token counts, gradient accumulation and a deterministic reference. Add DDP and measure synchronization and stragglers, then study FSDP2 gather/reduce-scatter against the per-rank HBM budget. Write a small size-sweep for all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter and all-to-all when multiple GPUs are available. Compare link/topology, synchronization, overlap and variance. Study TP, CP, PP and EP in that context and add one failure injection with a bounded timeout and a diagnosable trace. [PyTorch distributed](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/distributed.html) · [Flight Recorder](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/unstable/flight_recorder_tutorial.html) · [OpenAI/Microsoft networking case study](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/resilient-ai-supercomputer-networking-using-mrc-and-srv6.pdf)
5. **Turn generation into a serving engine.** Start with bounded page allocation and a per-request block table. Add a step scheduler with mixed prompt lengths, streaming, cancellation and page reclamation. Then compare static versus continuous batching and chunked prefill with repeated prefixes and a fixed memory budget. Test logits against the existing decoder and measure p50/p95 time to first token, inter-token latency, throughput and requests that meet latency targets. Include an overloaded trace to expose queueing. Then try compilation or CUDA Graphs with bounded shape buckets. [vLLM optimization guide](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/configuration/optimization/) · [vLLM metrics](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/design/metrics/) · [K3.1 Pro deployment](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/technical-articles/2026/kimi-k3-1-pro-on-amd-instinct-gpus.html)
6. **Bridge training and serving through a small rollout runtime.** Use a deterministic toy environment and a versioned policy. Compare synchronous rollouts with a bounded asynchronous queue, then add backpressure, timeout/retry behavior and a policy-staleness limit. Measure idle trainer time, rollout throughput, tail completion time and stale-sample rate. Keep rollout version metadata and recover an interrupted run without consuming a sample twice. The experiment complements the existing PPO/GRPO plan. [Kimi K3 §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24653) · [DeepSeek-V4.1 §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969) · [Reflection Beam](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam)

The first portfolio-quality result would be a reproducible training trace with a before/after memory and throughput comparison, gradient parity, and a short explanation of the remaining bottleneck. A second is the bounded-memory serving engine with a workload-driven benchmark. Cluster simulations and CPU tests can check semantics. NCCL overlap, topology claims and multi-GPU scaling require actual multi-GPU measurements.

## GPU kernel track beyond FlashAttention

| Order | Exercise | What to verify and explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Fused residual + RMSNorm**, then fused SiLU/gate | Reference parity across tail dimensions and strides. Measure bytes moved and achieved GB/s against separate ops. Add backward for one fused path. |
| 2 | **Tiled GEMM and fused epilogue** | Start with CUDA shared-memory tiling, then use a Tensor Core path or Triton/available library primitive. Compare square, skinny decode and non-aligned cases against cuBLAS/PyTorch. Explain coalescing, bank conflicts and register/occupancy tradeoffs. |
| 3 | **MoE token pack/unpack and grouped GEMM** | Reuse scans and permutation work. Test skewed expert loads, empty groups, repeated assignments, and source-slot parity. Compare gather/scatter bandwidth and loop versus grouped dispatch under the same route trace. |
| 4 | **Quantize/dequantize with block scales** | Test zero blocks, outliers, rounding and accumulation. Separate numerical error, byte footprint and throughput. Use BF16 and supported kernels on the RTX 4000 Ada. Emulate newer FP4 formats for correctness and reserve native Blackwell/MI355X performance claims for matching hardware. |
| 5 | **Paged KV append/gather and fused top-k selection** | Verify page boundaries, long prefixes and irregular request lengths. Compare bytes, scoreboard stalls and launch overhead with a dense reference. Then try streaming top-k without materializing the full score matrix, with exact-index checks and deterministic tie policy. |
| 6 | **Chunked recurrent update or persistent grouped work** | Pick KDA decode state update or a persistent grouped-GEMM scheduler. Compare batched versus single-token decode and separate numerical parity from hardware speed. Add graph capture only once shapes, allocation lifetimes and launch behavior are understood. |

Use a reference, deterministic correctness cases and repeatable profiling for each exercise. Real reports connect grouped GEMM, token permutation, quantization, recurrent state and sparse top-k to measurable bottlenecks. [Kimi K3 and MoonEP](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24653) · [DeepEP](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP) · [HyScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32057) · [Thinking Machines 4-bit training](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/end-to-end-4-bit-training/)
